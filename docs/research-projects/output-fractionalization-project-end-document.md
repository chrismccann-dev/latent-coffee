## What the project asked, and what it answered

**The question (roadmap § Next #9, scoped 2026-09-01):** separate bloom/body/tail fractions, understand each fraction's composition vs perception, recombine — output: a fractionalization taxonomy, *which coffees reward output selection and which don't*.

**The answer: the taxonomy exists, it is 2½-dimensional, and the project's real payload turned out to be a workflow, not a table.** Three tracks, three coffees, three different verdicts: tail-tax (curate concentrated + trace tail) · bloom-tax (hard bloom cut) · no tax (full cup wins). No two coffees shared a winning shape; nobody — operator or Assistant — ever predicted the convicted fraction ex ante (0/3). The durable output is the **fraction-curation workflow v1** (§ below): a probe-first, gate-cheap, blind-everything procedure that classifies a coffee and curates it when curation pays.

**The operator thesis that seeded the project** ("concentrated pour-over works because it keeps bloom + pour 1 and cuts the end off") was CONFIRMED as a mechanism on both coffees tested (T1 H1: a 1:10 cup landed at the front-recombination's exact TDS and character family; T2 reconfirmed the front-family read) — and REFINED as a preference: the operator's ideal was never a hard cut but a *re-proportion*, and even that is per-coffee.

**The RP8 carried question (removal vs dilution — same lever?):** distinct levers, three ways. At matched TDS, tail-removal reads brighter/crisper vs native concentration's deeper/rounder (T1); fraction composition changes a cup at identical strength (T2, T3's 0.83/0.83 pair); and dilution was the *weaker* lever where both were tried (T3 lengthening lost). Bypass-to-optimum remains sanctioned strength-targeting (RP8 canon, untouched); fraction curation is its own axis.

## Fraction-curation workflow v1 — THE PROJECT'S SHIPPABLE PAYLOAD

Carried verbatim from the T3 handoff brief (operator's 3-step synthesis, 2026-09-23, + the T3-earned gates). Runs on any valve/swap-cup brewer with cups + a scale; refractometer optional.

> **Step 0 — Seal a guess.** Write down which fraction you think carries the tax, and why. Don't revise after tasting. (Expect to be wrong; three coffees in, nobody has called it.)
>
> **Step 1 — Probe (1 dose).** Brew your lived recipe, but close the valve + swap cups + re-tare at each pour boundary so bloom, pour 1, and pour 2 land in separate cups. Three cups only, no flush. Taste each straight, cool-weighted. Name the most *volatile* fraction (sour / astringent / bitter / thin) and the *keeper base*. Note what each fraction *carries* (aromatics, sweetness, body), not just what it taxes. Then pour the remainders together and taste against your memory of the full cup — if it's a different coffee, your fraction brew went wrong.
>
> **Step 2 — Decide the cut depth BEFORE deciding the shape.** The volatile fraction is only a tax if the cup is better without it. Test that first and cheaply: brew the lived recipe twice, once as-is, once with the volatile fraction diverted to the side cup and ~2/3 of it poured back. Blind pair, cool-weighted. **If the full cup wins or it's a coin-flip, stop: this coffee does not reward selection.** You just saved the menu sitting.
>
> **Step 3 — Only if the shallow cut won: build the menu (1 dose).** Fraction-brew again. Build 2-3 cups from the keepers, named after their contents: one at natural strength (concentrate-and-emphasize), one with hot water added to a pre-computed strength ~0.2 below natural (lengthen-and-smooth), optionally one with the volatile fraction at trace. Shuffle, taste warm-to-cool, pick a winner. Dose minerals on the assembled cup, never on fractions.
>
> **Step 4 — Translate and replicate (2 doses).** Turn the winning build into a single-brew recipe by *matching its proportions* (divert-and-pour-back for the bloom; stop-at-weight for a tail cut). Brew it beside the full cup, blind pair. The real cup wins or the workflow didn't earn its keep.
>
> *Rules that cost us:* blind everything, including re-cupping to kill volume tells; sighted leans inverted twice. Draw TDS vials before dosing. Translate the winner faithfully first — sharpen the cut on a second pass, never on the translation. Rinse before the first cup.

**Validation status (Assistant's framing, operator to ratify at retro):** validated as a CLASSIFIER (three coffees, three correct classifications, incl. a clean negative); NOT validated as a cup-improver on a middle-case coffee — T3's curated cups lost to the full cup at two depths. The Step 2 gate exists so that result costs 2 vials next time, not 4. ~4-5 doses full run; 3 when the gate stops it.

## The arithmetic (settled, project-wide)

Per-fraction TDS × weight gives solids; recombination/curation TDS is predictable by weight-average (lengthened: solids ÷ (keeper mass + added water), verified). Gradient was monotonic front-to-tail on every clean brew (5/5; one inversion traced to a compromised pour). Measured lands BELOW predicted, consistently: **−0.05 to −0.14 on bench builds, to −0.21 on lived brews predicted from prior-day fraction reads** (P9-AI-1 CLOSED-as-characterized at T3; never isolated to evaporation vs aliquot vs meter). TDS equality between two cups is a gift: matched-TDS side-by-sides were the project's cleanest instrument (RP9-N4) — character differences at equal strength settled H3 (T1), the bloom-cut win (T2), and the inert-shallow-cut read (T3).
