# Coffees That Confirmed Hybrid

*Coffee Research · Latent · Brewing Historian cluster · by-strategy*

**Strategy definition:** see [BREWING.md § Axis 1 - Extraction Strategy](docs/skills/coordinator/catalog.md#brewing-domain-principles) for the canonical Hybrid definition (used when the brew is structured around phase boundaries with different jobs assigned to each phase, rather than a single extraction logic running throughout). Hybrid is the 6th first-class strategy (promoted v8.4, 2026-05-06) and ships with 5 sub-forms: Sequential / Phase-Mapped / Selective Bloom / Intensity-Clarity Split / Temperature-Staged. Hardware: Hario Switch at home (canonical) or SWORKS Bottomless Dripper at office when valve transitions are doing strategic work.

## Sequential (including the Graduated Taper)

**Canonical SWORKS Sequential = slow/slow/open:** Dial 0 closed bloom → Dial 5 restricted main pours (immersion-like contact window, builds aromatics + body) → Dial 7 open finish ("rinse, not steep" - cuts the tannic/over-extracted finish without giving back the body the restricted pours built). The inversion of Picolot's fast/fast/slow house structure is what makes it a Hybrid rather than Balanced Intensity. Three yeast-anaerobic / anaerobic-honey Gesha lots converged on this exact valve sequence (Janson 1010, Sebastian Ramirez White Honey, La Reserva Colibri - the last cracking to Dial 6 once the bed drops in Pour 2): it is the reference template for yeast-anaerobic lots on the office SWORKS when contact-time control matters more than intensity tuning. Straight naturals do NOT invert the valve structure (see the CCIL Controlled Natural note).

**Switch Sequential on a roast-forward lot (Newbery Khun Lao, medium Black Honey):** closed immersion builds sweetness off the percolation clock, open phase drains-and-cuts. Resolved as Sequential rather than Intensity-Clarity Split because the open phase's job was drain-and-cut, not clarity recovery. Two rules it established: (1) **the "both under-developed AND over-developed at once" signal is a Hybrid trigger, not a tweak trigger** - across a 6-brew single-mode arc no even-extraction lever could fix both ends, because more extraction feeds the roast body and less starves the fruit; pivot rather than iterate. (2) **On the Hario Switch the late cut and the open-drain are the same physical action** (closing the valve IS the cut) - the cleanest tail-cut hardware in the kit; cut tighter, not later (200g clamped the smoky finish where 210g let the tail through), and a shorter closed steep (1:30 → 1:20) reduces smoke at the source. (This is the canonical home for the Switch tail-cut note; [operational-reference.md](docs/skills/brewing-equipment-expert/cluster/operational-reference.md) Hario Switch row points here.) Roaster context: Newbery develops Thai coffees long for sweetness and published a ZeroWater recipe that was doing load-bearing suppression; home low-mineral water was materially better than office tap (CCIL Pattern #6).

**A temperature prior proven on plain percolation does not transfer to an immersion-front structure** (Latent Fazenda Um Pink Bourbon DRD, Batch 233): 95°C was proven on single-mode percolation, but the same 95°C under a closed bloom + closed Pour 1 over-extracted (woody, "overdone jasmine", fruit suppressed); 90°C on the unchanged structure resolved it in one move. When a pivot adds a closed immersion phase, re-set temperature ~4-5°C down first and leave grind alone. (The Untold roast of the same lot bled roast at 94°C under an ICS immersion front and held at 92°C - same direction, confounded there with steep length.)

**Graduated Taper (0→5→6→7)** - a distinct SWORKS shape from slow/slow/open, named by Chris at the 2026-07-27 arbitration (row in [sworks.md](docs/skills/brewing-equipment-expert/cluster/sworks.md) Canonical recipe patterns; classified `sequential` in the DB, the closest canonical sub-form). Hold-front / ease-mid / flush: Dial 0 closed bloom → Dial 5 restricted front-building window → Dial 6 half-open mid pour → Dial 7 open flush. Rules (Frank Aroste Washed Gesha 2026-07-22 + Tabaco Pata SL9 2026-09-14, both light washed high-altitude Peru/Cusco): (1) **the intermediate Dial 6 step is load-bearing** - Dial 5 through both mains over-built the body so it climbed over the attack; opening the mid sooner lets the body sit behind the front. (2) **The Dial-5 front-window length is the front/body balance lever** - 155g optimum on Aroste (130g thin and drifted; 220g body-heavy); tune ±10-15g before touching grind (valve-first doctrine; grind stayed at the SWORKS-calibrated 6.0 throughout). (3) Treat it as the **default SWORKS starting pattern for light washed high-altitude Peru**; the SL-lineage phase-separation risk flagged for fast cones did not materialize on the valve - the restricted front window is the structural fix - and a hot-cup "oversteeped" read can be a transient that integrates warm/cool, so judge the whole arc before shortening the window. Trigger shape on Aroste: fast flat papers (Sibarist FLAT FAST, xBloom Premium) drained before the front could build and finer grind could not fix a filter-flow bottleneck - a per-phase contact-time problem only the valve addresses (Sey ultra-light under-extraction presents as hollow/thin, not bitter).

## Intensity-Clarity Split (ICS)

**Architecture:** Phase 1 closed immersion (reach a buried register, build front body) → Phase 2 open percolation (finish). The de-facto default Hybrid sub-form whenever the cup says "reach one register while not over-extracting another". Its purpose broadened with each lot and now spans **five problem shapes**: roast-tail suppression (Untold WWNAT, Janson Pacamara, Finca Deborah; Bukure dark-tea overhang; NO°5 medium Bourbon), process-derived front/back imbalance (SG El Placer heavy anaerobic honey, light roast), buried-producer-note recovery on an underdeveloped or clean-tail cup (Latent Mount Elgon one-shot; Untold Fazenda Um Pink Bourbon), aromatic-ceiling recovery when the lot cups quiet (Terraform Clara Luz Sidra - CCIL Pattern #7), and SL-lineage / aromatic-landrace phase separation that the passive April vehicle alone could not integrate (Snite SL28; Glitch 74165 - CCIL Pattern #4). The original co-ferment-muddiness hypothesis remains untested. Common thread: one register buried that immersion reaches, while the open half either drains a loud register or just finishes clean.

**Triggers:** the simultaneous under-front AND over-back signal; fruit-on-attack + ashy/smoky/oversteeped-tea body (developed roast - [by-roast-level/medium-developed.md](docs/skills/brewing-historian/cluster/patterns/by-roast-level/medium-developed.md)); a cup that reads muted on the brew AND on a cupping. **Run a single-mode diagnostic brew first and read the back half:** heavy/ashy back = **drain shape** (fast paper + real cut); clean back with a front that fades on cooling = **recovery shape** (medium paper, tiny or no cut). When a Hybrid pivot is a live possibility, run the diagnostic Brew 1 on the Switch with the valve held open (mechanically a V60) so the pivot changes only valve behavior, not brewer geometry (Fazenda Um PB).

**Vehicle + paper routing rule** (Finca Deborah 2026-07-27 - the first ICS resolved by a hardware correction after three April Switch brews spent grind, temperature and a cut against an architecture that was never running; confirmed bare over a sink: the April Switch drains far slower than the Hario/V60 Switch):
- **Developed roast with a tail to flush → cone switch (Hario) + genuinely fast paper (Sibarist CONE FAST, measured ~45s).** The open phase is defined as a true RINSE; a slow flat switch turns it into continued extraction and steeps the heavy register back in. "Fast" on the spec sheet is not fast in the cup - the Hario V60 Meteor 02 measured ~65s and failed on the Pacamara; pair the paper to the MEASURED drawdown. The April Switch's integration strength is a liability when a register needs draining rather than blending ("much more integrated, but darker"). All three Untold roast-tail resolutions (WWNAT, Pacamara, Deborah) ran cone + fast paper.
- **Light roast / recovery shape → April Switch + April Paper, or Hario Switch + a medium cone (CONE B3, ~60s).** Integration and a gentle finish are the point; a fast rinse starves a back half that still holds extractable fruit/body (Component Terra in hindsight; Fazenda Um PB prospectively - CONE FAST was deliberately NOT reached for). All light-roast April Switch data points (Bukure, Clara Luz, Snite SL28, El Paraiso Red Plum, Glitch 74165) fit this.
- **On the April Switch, closed-through-Pour-1 (~150g held in immersion) is the canonical ICS execution**, not closed-bloom-only - the spec drifted toward Selective Bloom twice and the closed-through-Pour-1 execution won both cups (Bukure, Clara Luz); the larger immersed volume is what reaches the buried register. The flat bed drains slower than a cone: coarsen (6.5 → 6.7), kettle off base, clamp the cut tight; April FAST paper is the held-in-reserve faster-drain lever.
- **A genuinely fast paper collapses a single-steep-then-one-open ICS into plain immersion** (Gesha Clouds Brew 1 drained in 10-15s through CONE FAST: thin + sour). On a fast paper with no tail to rinse, a real split needs two discrete immersion → drain phases.
- **Office SWORKS ICS:** Dial 0 closed bloom → Dial 5 restricted build → Dial 7 fast open drain + lift-cut (the lift substitutes for the Switch's valve-close; slightly less precise, still clamped NO°5's tail at ~180g).

**The cut is a dial, not a binary - cut size tracks how much heavy fraction there is:**
- **Roast tail: 40-55g discarded, tighter beats later** - Newbery 200/240, Pacamara 205/250, Bukure 185/240 (195 let the tail through), NO°5 180/225. **Taste the discard glass:** markedly darker/roastier than the kept cup = the cut is removing overhang and working, clamp tighter for more; a weaker version of the cup's core = the cut has gone past overhang into core, back off. When the heavy register is one of the producer's OWN notes (Bukure: black tea is the first published note), the cut strips the over-extracted overhang, not the note.
- **Clean tail: tiny (~13g of 240g, Fazenda Um PB - the terminal fraction still tasted roasty in isolation) or none.** On a light roast the late drain carries balancing depth: Mount Elgon's accidental cut-and-recombine proved the slow tail held the entire back half (full ~5-min choked drawdown did not over-extract); Snite SL28 and Clara Luz tested a mild cut and rejected it (cup over-separated, lemon over-expressed); Deborah at WB 60.6 cut 200/250 and the roast stood proportionally MORE naked. The cut-vs-no-cut boundary sits between Agtron ~48 (cut tight) and ~60 (do not cut). Tasting the slow final drip separately mid-brew is the generalizable diagnostic - same move, opposite conclusions on heavy vs clean tails.

**Parameter levers, in order:**
- **Coarsen for the rinse** so the open phase drains rather than extracts (6.0 → 6.3 WWNAT; 6.5 → 6.8 Pacamara; 6.5 → 6.7 Bukure on the flat) - OR let the brewer/filter flow profile substitute for the grind move (El Placer held 6.4 on Switch + Abaca+ Cup 4 and the fast drain still worked).
- **Open-phase timing is the primary tail-control lever** on the drain shape (El Placer: opening at 1:40 rather than 1:50 kept the back half clean).
- **Phase-1 steep time is the front-reach lever, and the integration point is per-lot, not a sub-form constant:** Mount Elgon 1:45; Fazenda Um PB 1:45 landed where 2:00 read as two phases that would not integrate; Helm Mystic 2:00 (1:50 under, 2:10 disintegrated). The disintegration-on-too-long signal is confirmed on two lots.
- **Heat buys reach in Phase 1 while the fast drain keeps the mobilized roast from concentrating** (NO°5 ran 92°C where the roast level suggested 88-90) - but on a developed roast with delicate aromatics heat does NOT recruit fruit (Deborah: a clean 95°C Phase 1 produced no added sweetness and more roast), and **coarse plus cool starves fruit faster than it starves roast** (6.8 / 89°C killed the attack while roast persisted) - Suppression is not a safe default there. Do not assume an Agtron-based temperature ceiling on a coffee whose variety baseline runs lighter: Clara Luz at 62.4 held 94°C flat with no roast bleed; the 91°C "protect against roast" instinct was suppressing fruit.
- Push the immersion hard for underdeveloped recovery (Mount Elgon: 96°C / 6.2 / open 1:45 lifted the producer notes clear of the almond with zero over-extraction).

**Honest ceiling:** at Agtron 47.9 (Pacamara) and 60.6 (Deborah) the roast-derived register is inherent; ICS RELOCATES it beneath the fruit rather than eliminating it. "Best dark-roast cup of the arc" is a relocation win.

## Temperature-Staged

**Architecture:** the temperature change is bound to the phase boundary - hot Phase 1 for front reach, cooler Phase 2 for a clean tail. Four confirmations (Component Terra Pink Bourbon anaerobic natural 2026-06-05, Latent Gesha Clouds 2026-06-14, Latent El Paraiso Red Plum 2026-06-25, Glitch Tamiru Alo 74165 2026-07-02); **all four were reached via an ICS lineage** - the ICS build fixed the attack and then exposed a within-cup front/back temperature gradient - so **the clean standalone bittersweet-cliff trigger (thin at 95°C / bitter at 96°C on an otherwise even cup) remains untested.**

**Trigger:** the two phases want DIFFERENT temperatures - the front is under-reached and needs heat, but that same heat pushes the back half into a drying tail or (Glitch) an off-peak collapse to burnt dark chocolate. This emerges LAYERED on an ICS build, not only as the clean cliff.

**Execution rules:**
- **Two kettles.** Hold each phase temperature exactly (95-96°C Phase 1 / 88-90°C Phase 2) rather than letting one kettle decline - duration does not compensate for falling temperature (Gesha Clouds Brew 1's long single steep ran cool the whole way and read thin + sour despite the contact). Flagged a keeper technique at first use.
- **Temperature is the front-end reach lever on light anaerobic naturals** (Component Terra: 95°C fixed the attack where 92-93°C and grind could not; Gesha Clouds 96°C; Red Plum 96°C). Grind stays secondary.
- **On a light roast with no tail, Phase 2 is a clean-finish + aromatic-preservation job, not a tail drain - drain fully, no cut** (all three light data points). On CONE FAST, convert Phase 2 to a second closed immersion so steep time, not paper flow, controls the back half (Component Terra); two discrete closed steeps are what deliver a split on a fast paper (Gesha Clouds).
- **Phase balance (water allocation) is the load-bearing lever and moves front/back directly** - 150g hot / 75g cool read back-heavy with a hollow middle (the large cool phase amplified rose/lemongrass and diluted the front); ~185g hot / ~40g cool restored tangerine/kiwi/sweetness up front and filled the middle. When a Temperature-Staged or ICS cup reads back-heavy with a hollow middle, rebalance water toward the hot phase before touching grind or temp.
- **De-mud first, then re-diagnose (Red Plum, valve brewer):** a cup that reads flat + darker-tea + muted-fruit all at once on a LIGHT roast is MUDDY-flat (over-immersion), not hollow - fix valve before grind (closed bloom ≤25s, open earlier: 1:45 → ~1:08). Only once the mud clears can you tell whether the residual is a thin front; Temperature-Staged is then the surgical front-intensity lever that does not re-heavy the freshly-cleaned back. Residual mid-palate body on that lot was held by water chemistry (2 drops Apax Tonik + 2 Jamm), not a brewing lever - the first Hybrid brew where a mineral add was the load-bearing final move (research lead; the non-water route, finer or longer dwell, risks re-muddying).
- **Tart diagnostic:** tart that FLATTENS on cooling = under-extraction; tart that HOLDS or sweetens on cooling = the lot's fruit character (Component Terra's guava).

## Still-open candidates

- **Hybrid (Phase-Mapped) re-framing of the Tamarind reference** - re-document the Moonwake El Eden Tamarind Full Expression recipe (Dial 5 through mains, crack to Dial 6 midway through Pour 3) as saturation / body-building / clarity-finish phases; hypothesis: explicit role-mapping at brief time surfaces failure modes (premature transition → thin body) earlier. Compare iteration efficiency on the next heavy co-ferment lot.
- **Hybrid (Selective Bloom) on an aromatic Clarity-First lot** (Eline Ferket 2025 pattern) - bloom liquid separated, evaluated, recombined or discarded. Highest leverage on an Esmeralda climate-controlled natural or clean washed Gesha.
- **Standalone Temperature-Staged trigger** (see above) and the **co-ferment-muddiness ICS shape** (the original hypothesis; the Jeferson Motta heavy anaerobic washed Colombian Gesha is still untested).

> The Picolot Emerald PL#015 Mokka Natural reference recipe is **NOT classified as Hybrid** despite its Dial 0 / 7 / 7 / 5 valve work - the bloom immersion and slow restricted finish are recipe details, not phase boundaries that change extraction logic mid-brew; the brew is Full Expression at fast-flow parameters. Strategy classification is about intent, not whether a valve transition exists.
