# Cross-Coffee Insight Layer

*Coffee Research · Latent · Roasting Historian cluster · patterns*

*This section is updated at lot close-out. It captures patterns that emerge across multiple coffees, not within a single lot. These insights reduce the number of experiments needed on future lots by providing informed starting hypotheses.*

> Current data set: 4 fully resolved counterflow lots (CGLE Sudan Rume Hybrid Washed, ref #133; CGLE Mandela XO, ref #139; CGLE Sudan Rume Natural, closed 2026-05-23, ref #187; Costa Rica Higuito Anaerobic Dry Process, closed 2026-05-19, ref #185), several active counterflow lots (Gesha Clouds, Wush Wush Natural, Bukure Natural Lot 21, El Paraíso Red Plum Castillo), and 2 pre-counterflow closed lots. ## Confirmed Patterns

> Cross-lot-validated patterns at High confidence per [CONTEXT-roasting.md § Key-insight confidence ladder](CONTEXT-roasting.md). These are the stable, multi-lot-tested patterns Chris's V1-design heuristics rest on. Detail and evidence live in the sections below; this is the navigable index. Promotion criterion: High confidence AND repeated across ≥2 lots OR strong cross-lot corroboration. Maintenance cadence: [ARBITER.md § CCIL consolidation pass](ARBITER.md#ccil-consolidation-pass).

- **Every coffee has an FC floor and a drop-temp ceiling; drop ceilings cluster at 205-208°C across counterflow lots tested**, varying by processing method (XO-fermented lower; high-density washed higher). The FC floor is a coffee-specific architectural minimum below which the cup reads underdeveloped regardless of dev time. Evidence: 4 lots tested in counterflow (Sudan Rume Washed, Sudan Rume Natural, Mandela XO, Gesha Washed). Detail: [§ FC Floor & Ceiling by Processing Method](#fc-floor--ceiling-by-processing-method).
- **First roast of a session runs late and hot vs. subsequent roasts on identical protocol.** Apply thermal-reset discipline (dry roast to 140°C); fall back to 128°C hopper load if replication is critical. The third roast of a session is the most structurally consistent. Evidence: multiple lots, multiple sessions. Detail: [§ Session Position Effect - Machine-Specific Data](#session-position-effect---machine-specific-data).
- **Day 7 pourover is the only reliable evaluation gate** across washed / natural / XO-fermented lots in Chris's archive. Day 4 cupping is unreliable or actively misleading; Day 6 is directionally reliable but Day 7 remains the standard. Evidence: 20+ batches Sudan Rume Washed + 10 batches Sudan Rume Natural + 13 batches Mandela XO. Detail: [§ Rest Behavior Patterns](#rest-behavior-patterns).
- **The xbloom evaluation recipe systematically under-extracts delicate aromatic washed coffees**; the evaluation recipe is calibrated for comparison consistency, not expression maximization. An optimized brew session (typical lever: 91°C / 1:14, ~3°C cooler and ~3 points more concentrated than the eval recipe) is required before declaring a lot resolved. Evidence: confirmed on Sudan Rume Washed, replicated on Mandela XO. Detail: [§ Rest Behavior Patterns](#rest-behavior-patterns) (Brew recipe gap).
- **WB-to-ground Agtron delta is the most sensitive internal development signal so far.** First experiment sets on any coffee will show large deltas; shrinking delta over successive V-sets is a reliable signal that profile development is on the right track. Tight delta strongly correlates with best-cup quality at resolution. Evidence: confirmed across Sudan Rume Washed (#119 delta 1.0), Mandela XO (#139 delta 3.6), Anaerobic Dry Process Higuito v1c (delta 1.3 - winner). Detail: [§ WB-to-Ground Agtron Delta Norms by Processing Method](#wb-to-ground-agtron-delta-norms-by-processing-method).
- **Sudan Rume aromatic vocabulary is shared across washed and natural processing** (jasmine / bergamot / lemongrass / ginger underneath sweetness, with stone-fruit tartness on washed and tart sweetness + spice on natural). "Fermented / funky" descriptors on this variety usually equal underdevelopment, not defect - cross-reference producer tasting notes and seek a reference roast before assuming a roast artifact. Evidence: Sudan Rume Washed (closed) + Sudan Rume Natural (V1-V3, 10 batches) + same farm (CGLE) shared lemongrass signature. Detail: [§ Varietal Aromatic Fingerprints](#varietal-aromatic-fingerprints).
- **High-density washed Colombian (≥800 g/L) is energy-tolerant**; anchor V1 on CF-Light or equivalent confirmed profile and expect FC floor near 200°C, drop ceiling near 207°C. Evidence: Sudan Rume Washed (809 g/L). (Sudan Rume Natural at 791 g/L sits below the ≥800 threshold and is natural-processed — it does not corroborate this washed pattern.) Detail: [§ Green Spec → Starting Hypothesis](#green-spec--starting-hypothesis).
- **Producer notes including "lemongrass" / "jasmine" / "bergamot" predict that the standard evaluation recipe will under-extract**; plan a pushed brew session (91-92°C, 1:14) and evaluate at the cool stage. Do not conclude the roast failed if the cup is muted at 94°C / 1:17. Evidence: confirmed on Sudan Rume Washed; carries to delicate aromatic coffees broadly. Detail: [§ Green Spec → Starting Hypothesis](#green-spec--starting-hypothesis).
- **On heavy-anaerobic naturals, reach target core development via the bean-temp end-condition target (drop point) on a fixed curve, not via peak inlet or post-peak decline shape - and set the bean-temp target ~3-4°C above the observed FC temp so a clean auto-drop extends the post-FC window rather than firing right behind FC.** Confidence: High (CGLE-GESHA-CLOUDS-2026, resolved across four V-sets at close-out 2026-06-14). The lot wanted markedly MORE core development than its washed anchor predicted; peak-inlet sweeps (V1/V3) and post-peak decline (V2) were the wrong/non-levers, and the resolution came from raising the bean-temp auto-drop target from 209°C (fired too early, forced manual holds) to 211°C (clean auto-drop, dev ~34s) on a fixed 250°C-peak curve. Corollary (also High): on this lot the xbloom gate systematically FLATTERED the surface and mis-ranked vs real pourover (V3's 191 was an xbloom finalist but collapsed to body-centric dark-tea at the Simulated Pourover Gate) - confirm the leading slot at a real-pourover gate (SPG or optimized-brew dial-in) before the reference call. This corroborates the xbloom-dark-tea-ceiling pattern seen on COS-HIG-BOR-2026 and RWA-NOVA-NAT21-RB-2026. Detail: closed-lot learnings [learnings/cgle-gesha-clouds-2026.md](docs/skills/roasting-historian/cluster/learnings/cgle-gesha-clouds-2026.md).

- **The xbloom evaluation gate misranks close candidates on anaerobic-natural / heavy-ferment lots; run a Balanced Intensity real-pourover arbiter on the top 2 before declaring an outcome.** The gate is not biased one way: COS-HIG-BOR produced two reversals + one confirmation (non-deterministic between close candidates); RWA-NOVA-AN10 produced two reversals that both over-amplified the brighter/less-developed slot (producer-consistent brightness bias). Evidence: 7 instances across 4 lots - 5 slot-level (COS-HIG-BOR + RWA-NOVA-AN10), a sixth extending scope beyond anaerobic to engineered-process naturals generally (BRA-FAZENDAUM-PB-DRD dark-room-dried, 2026-08-20), and the first inversion at the final REFERENCE-CALL level (PAN-DEBORAH-GEISHA-NAT yeast-inoculated natural Gesha, 2026-09-07); promoted 2026-08-08. Detail: [§ xbloom Evaluation Gate Misranking on Engineered-Process Naturals](#xbloom-evaluation-gate-misranking-on-engineered-process-naturals).

## FC Floor & Ceiling by Processing Method

Every coffee appears to have a minimum FC temp below which the cup is underdeveloped regardless of dev time, and a maximum drop temp above which overdevelopment suppresses aromatics. These windows are coffee-specific and must be found empirically, but processing method appears to set the rough range.

| Process Type | FC Floor (est.) | Drop Ceiling (est.) | Evidence Base | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| High-density washed Colombian (Sudan Rume) | ~200°C | ~208°C | CGLE-SRUME-WASHED-2026 (20+ batches) | High - confirmed empirically |
| Natural (Sudan Rume, CGLE farm) | FC ambiguous/inaudible - bean temp end condition is the correct management signal. Bean temp target 204-205°C. Total time ceiling 6:15. Drop above 208°C produces overdevelopment signal. 242°C peak + hard cliff required - 240°C peak insufficient. | CGLE-SRUME-NATURAL-2026 (V1-V5, 19 batches, lot closed) | High - lot closed, reference roast confirmed. FC-based management retired; bean temp end condition is primary. |
| XO-fermented Colombian | ~200°C (est.) | ~205-206°C | CGLE-MANDELA-XO-2026 (13 batches, 4 experiment sets) | Medium - lot closed. FC floor estimated from RoR flattening; drop ceiling confirmed - above 208°C produces overdevelopment signal. Best results at 203-205°C drop temp with inaudible FC. |
| Washed Gesha (counterflow) | ~200°C (est.) | Unknown | GV-OMA-25-035 (3 counterflow batches) | Very low - green exhausted before resolution |

**Key pattern emerging:** Drop temp ceiling appears to sit around 205-208°C across all coffees tested in counterflow, varying by process. XO-fermented coffees appear to have a lower optimal drop temp (203-205°C) than washed high-density lots (206-207°C). FC floor on XO-fermented is difficult to establish empirically because FC is acoustically absent - manage by RoR behavior and bean temp.

**Bean temp end condition as primary management signal for ambiguous-FC lots (High confidence):** Confirmed across XO-fermented (Mandela XO) and traditional natural Ethiopian landrace (Sudan Rume Natural, V1-V5 lot closed). Dev time and FC timestamp are unreliable drop signals on these lots. Set bean temp end condition in the Roest profile at 205°C with a 6:15 total-time safety net.


**Red Bourbon (Rwanda Northern Province, high altitude natural)** - FC floor ~203°C / 238°C peak inlet on L200 counterflow at this density/moisture (799 g/L, 10.10%). Below 238°C peak = bean does NOT reach FC: confirmed empirically at v2a (Batch 193, 234°C peak) where the bean topped 199.9°C and the roast ran to the 6:00 profile terminus with 0 cracks. At 238°C peak (v2b, the lot reference Batch 194) FC arrives marginally at 05:27 / 203.1°C with 1 crack (subtle audibility). **The designed 207°C drop-ceiling bean_temp end-condition was structurally unreachable** at v2b's energy level (bean topped 203.1°C); use bean_temp end-condition 203-205°C as the operative trigger on this cultivar/process at this altitude.

**Important reframe - FC threshold ≠ drinkability threshold on this cultivar/process at this altitude.** v2a (sub-FC, 0 cracks) cupped as flat-with-brightness, NOT catastrophically green/grassy as predicted from structural sub-FC underdev. The cup floor sits BELOW the FC floor on this lot - useful counter for future low-energy probes on similar lots: don't assume sub-FC = unusable cup. Underdevelopment manifests as structural FLATNESS (missing acidity/body/finish staging) rather than off-flavor (green/grassy/sour).

*Source:* RWA-NOVA-NAT21-RB-2026 V1 + V2 (6 batches), lot closed 2026-06-06. First East African Red Bourbon natural in counterflow archive. Confidence: High for the 238°C FC threshold + bean_temp end-condition 203-205°C finding (empirically confirmed across v2a vs v2b boundary); Medium for the cup-floor-below-FC-floor reframe (single-lot N=1, but the v2a-vs-prediction divergence is direct evidence and the reframe is more about NOT applying the standard sub-FC = green/grassy heuristic on similar lots).

**Red Bourbon (Rwanda Northern Province) - mild ANAEROBIC natural extension (RWA-NOVA-AN10-RB-2026, lot closed 2026-08-08):** at higher energy than the Lot 21 baseline, FC becomes genuinely audible on this cultivar: 236°C peak = no FC (bean tops out sub-FC), 239 = 1 pop, 242 = audible (2-11 cracks across V1/V2). At 242 peak with a sustained post-FC inlet tail (239/235/231/228 at 4:00/5:00/6:00/7:00), FC arrives ~4:28-4:31 / 203.8-204.7°C and bean-temp auto-drop targets up to 211°C are reachable under genuine dev-phase energy (RoR 10.7-11.1 at 4:00, no bake). Drop ceiling: 209°C fully clean (zero overdev signs - lot reference Batch 218, SPG-validated) / 211°C conventionally overdeveloped ("too roasted, too far", fruit flattened, NO XO-style spice - mild ferments overdev conventionally). Ceiling brackets 209-211, a full 4-6°C above the XO-family ~205-206 ceiling: do NOT import heavy-ferment ceilings onto mild anaerobics. Steep color slope on this curve family: ~3 Agtron points per °C of drop above 207 (83.9 / 79.3 / 72.1 across the 207/209/211 ladder). Secondary observation: crack count tracked time-past-FC-onset almost linearly (0/4/11 cracks at ~15/40/80s), reading as a dev-time signal rather than an energy signal. Confidence: High for the 242 audibility threshold + the 209-211 ceiling bracket (empirically probed, lot closed); exact ceiling position between 209 and 211 unprobeable - green exhausted.

## WB-to-Ground Agtron Delta Norms by Processing Method

The WB-to-ground delta is the most sensitive internal development signal identified so far. Target ranges appear to vary meaningfully by processing method due to differences in how the bean's outer layers develop relative to the core.

| Process Type | Target Delta | V1 Typical Delta | Resolution Delta | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Washed (high density) | ≤3 points | 3-5 points (V1/V2) | 1.0 points (#119) | Tight delta strongly correlated with best cup quality |
| Natural (fruit layer, Sudan Rume CGLE) | Delta ≤6 recommended as signal of adequate core development | 7-11 points (V1) | 3.1 points (#169 V4C) / 10.6 points (#187 V5A reference roast) | Fruit layer provides thermal insulation - large deltas expected early. Both batches with delta ≤3 (V4C) and delta ~10 (V5A) produced viable cups at the right brew recipe. xbloom gate underrepresents batches with large deltas; real pourover required. LOT CLOSED. |
| XO-fermented | ≤4 points (est.) | Not systematically measured in V1 | 3.6 points (#139, WB 76 / ground 72.4 — NB: not reproducible from the structured record; `ground_agtron` is unrecorded on every Mandela XO cupping, so this figure is prose-only / estimated. Verify.) | Fermentation layer behaves similarly to natural in early experiment sets - large deltas expected when profile is not yet dialed. |

**Key pattern:** First experiment sets on any coffee will likely show large deltas as the profile is dialed in. Shrinking delta over successive experiment sets is a reliable signal that profile development is on the right track.

**Negative delta on washed (Low confidence, single seed point — washed one-shot, 2026-06):** the archive's first LARGE negative WB-to-ground delta (−6.2; ground markedly LIGHTER than whole bean) landed on a washed one-shot and read as a surface-ran-ahead-of-core underdevelopment signature. Scope this to washed / high-density for now and keep it distinct from the SMALL negatives seen on heavy-ferment naturals (v2b −0.3 / v2c −1.1 on COS-HIG-BOR-2026, where sign carried cooling-stage information but did NOT predict the winner — see the Anaerobic Dry Process entry below). On washed coffees the prose/norm patterns otherwise assume delta > 0, so a large sign flip is the cleanest single-number underdevelopment flag here. One data point — promote toward a norm only if a second washed lot pairs a large negative delta with underdevelopment.

**Ground Agtron vs Whole-Bean Agtron as extraction prior (Medium-High confidence):** Confirmed on CGLE Sudan Rume Natural close-out - whole-bean Agtron (78.7, light) incorrectly suggested pushing harder at brew time; ground Agtron (68.1, medium-light) correctly predicted over-extraction risk and correctly set the temperature-down direction. For coffees with delta above ~6 points, always use ground Agtron to set the extraction strategy prior.

**Anaerobic Dry Process (heavy fermentation, 2-3 day sealed) - resolved at lot close (COS-HIG-BOR-2026, 2026-05-19):** Target delta ≤3 points (Medium-High confidence, single lot fully resolved across V1-V3). The reference roast v3b (#185) landed at delta 2.8 - inside the target zone with both WB (77.3) and ground (74.5) shifted together rather than surface running ahead of core. The original V1-only ≤2 hypothesis was slightly too tight: across the full lot, the magnitude that mattered was |delta| ≤3 (v1c 1.3 won V1; v2b -0.3 won V2; v3b 2.8 won the lot). A key refinement landed at close: the SIGN of the delta carries cooling-stage information at the xbloom gate but does NOT predict the real-pourover winner - v2b (-0.3) and v2c (-1.1) both negative, only v2b won; v3a (+4.4, surface well ahead) flattened on cooling and lost. Large positive delta (surface ahead of core) correlates with the underdev signal (aggressive-alcoholic-separated; v1a +4.2, v3a +4.4); magnitude ≤3 is the development-quality target. Higher peak inlet produced TIGHTER delta early - heavy anaerobic fermentation + fruit-layer thermal insulation requires more energy to drive core development in step with surface.

**Nova Red Bourbon family (mild anaerobic + clean natural) - widest-delta-wins polarity (Medium-High confidence, 4 consecutive V-set instances across 2 lots, recorded 2026-08-05):** on the Nova Washing Station Red Bourbon lots (RWA-NOVA-NAT21-RB-2026 + RWA-NOVA-AN10-RB-2026), the slot with the WIDEST WB->Gnd delta has aligned with the preferred cup four consecutive times, culminating in AN10's SPG-validated reference candidate Batch 218 (delta +6.3; AN10 V1's SPG winner 216 was widest at +6.9). This is the opposite polarity from the washed tight-delta norm and from the |delta| <= 3 heavy-anaerobic target (COS-HIG-BOR-2026) - scope it to this producer/cultivar family (lower-density Red Bourbon, mild ferment) rather than generalizing to anaerobic naturals broadly. Practical read at the gate: on these lots a wide delta is NOT an underdevelopment flag, and the narrow-delta slot has repeatedly been the one that collapses under real extraction (AN10 217 at +2.8: bright at the gate, hollow/separated at the SPG).

## Session Position Effect - Machine-Specific Data

Confirmed across multiple lots and sessions: first roast of a session consistently runs slower and produces later, hotter FC than subsequent roasts on identical protocol. This effect is consistent enough to predict:

| Session Position | FC Timing vs. Target | Recommended Correction | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| First roast of session | Late by ~10-15s, ~1-2°C hotter | None - rely on thermal reset protocol (dry roast to 140°C). Fallback: 128°C hopper load if replication-critical. | Most variable roast in any session - consider warm-up function |
| Second roast | Close to target, slight improvement | Standard protocol (125°C hopper load) | Reliable, consistent |
| Third roast | On target or very slightly faster | Standard protocol | Most consistent - closest to established targets |

**Practical implication for experiment design:** When designing replication sessions, the third roast of the session is the most structurally consistent. When designing experiments with three A/B/C batches, batch C will naturally run slightly faster and hotter than batch A on identical protocol - factor this into interpretation of results.

## Green Spec → Starting Hypothesis

Translates density, moisture, processing method, and variety into directional cues for V1 design. Use this as a starting hypothesis only - every new coffee should still test energy direction through a three-batch V1 before committing to a profile.

**Two distinct arbitration mechanisms operate in the same V1 design pass** ([CONTEXT-roasting.md § Signal precedence](CONTEXT-roasting.md) + [§ Multi-factor weighting](CONTEXT-roasting.md)):

- The **additive table** governs continuous numerical adjustments (peak inlet hedge, fan-curve modifications, V1 spread width, Maillard % tolerance) where multiple signals contribute hedged adjustments that sum.
- The **precedence table** governs categorical / discrete decisions (which anchor profile, which end condition mechanism, which direction the energy adjustment runs) where one signal wins outright over the others.

When a precedence rule fires, it overrides the additive-table guidance on that specific decision — read the precedence table first, then walk the additive table for everything precedence didn't already decide.

### Additive table — continuous adjustments

| Signal | Starting Hypothesis | Confidence & Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Density ≥ 800 g/L (high density)** | More energy-tolerant. Anchor peak inlet on CF-Light or equivalent confirmed profile. Expect FC floor near 200°C. | High - confirmed on Sudan Rume Washed (809 g/L). Sudan Rume Natural (791 g/L) is below the ≥800 threshold and natural-processed, so it is not corroborating evidence here. |
| **Density ≤ 760 g/L (low density)** | Taper energy earlier. Reduce peak inlet by 3-5°C vs. CF-Light. Compress Maillard slightly. Watch for overdevelopment. | Low - no directly resolved low-density counterflow lot yet. Treat as starting hypothesis only. |
| **Moisture ≥ 11% (high moisture)** | Longer drying phase needed. Hold 01:15 and 02:30 inlet higher to complete drying before Maillard. Expect later FC if energy is normal. Critical nuance: raise *drying* energy to match the curve traced on a normal-moisture lot - **do not conflate with more development**. High moisture needs drying support; it does not automatically mean more post-crack dev. | Medium-High - counterflow has not yet seen a high-moisture lot in Chris's archive, but [peer livestream confirmation (Dongzhe, 2026-05-17)](docs/roasting/dongzhe-livestream-2026-05.md): he raised overall drying power on an ~11% moisture Cascade Fermentation to match prior-roast momentum, deliberately separated from late Maillard / development decisions. Same machine + same counterflow mode. |
| **Moisture ≤ 10% (low moisture)** | Shorter drying phase. FC arrives faster. Gentler / slower early energy to avoid overshoot and runaway. Watch for compressed dev time. Drop ceiling more important than usual. | Medium-High - confirmed on Sudan Rume Natural (10.3%); carries to Gesha Clouds (10.4%) as active hypothesis. [Peer livestream confirmation (Dongzhe, 2026-05-17)](docs/roasting/dongzhe-livestream-2026-05.md): explicit "approach it a little bit slower" on an 8.9-9% moisture Precursor Amplification Natural to avoid overshoot. Same machine + same counterflow mode. |
| **Natural with visible fruit layer — flavor-quality hedge** | Once FC timing is workable (see precedence table for the energy-direction decision), dial energy *back* rather than forward. Sudan Rume Natural V1-V3 (10 batches): 247°C peak was needed to get FC timing into range, but higher energy amplified an unwanted drying/astringent compound. The preferred cup came from the lowest-energy batch (Batch 152, no audible FC, Maillard 66.9%, extended total time). Do NOT assume "natural = less heat" for FC timing, but DO assume "natural = less heat" for flavor quality on Sudan Rume-type naturals. | Medium-High - confirmed on Sudan Rume Natural V1-V3 (10 batches). The energy-vs-flavor tension is now well-established for this specific variety/process combination. |
| **Heavy anaerobic / extended fermentation (>36hr) — continuous adjustments** | Fan floor slightly lower (63-65% in Maillard). Manage drop primarily by bean temp and RoR flattening (FC mark is unreliable when audibility is silent). Maillard % tolerance widens to ~48% before flagging (per [CONTEXT-roasting.md § Maillard %](CONTEXT-roasting.md) — calculation depends on an upstream FC mark). End-condition mechanism is a categorical decision and lives in the precedence table. | Medium-High - confirmed on Mandela XO (silent crack across all 4 experiment sets), active hypothesis for Gesha Clouds (84hr anaerobic) |
| **Variety = Gesha (any process, counterflow)** | Dev time floor of 48s minimum. Use shaped fan curve from start. Be skeptical of any batch with dev time under 40s regardless of Agtron. | Medium - GV Oma Washed 40s confirmed as floor (3 underdeveloped batches), active hypothesis for Gesha Clouds |
| **Producer notes include "lemongrass," "jasmine," "bergamot" (delicate aromatic signals)** | Evaluation recipe will likely under-extract. Plan pushed brew for optimized session (91-92°C, 1:14 ratio). At Day 7, evaluate at cool stage, not hot. Do not conclude roast failed if muted at 94°C/1:17. | High - confirmed on Sudan Rume Washed via optimized brew session |

**How to use the additive table:** Read every row whose Signal applies to the new coffee. Combine the Starting Hypotheses additively into V1 design — density / moisture / altitude contributions stack cleanly, and the resulting peak inlet hedge / fan adjustment is the operator-judgment composite of the contributing rows. Signals can stack (e.g. high density + low moisture + Gesha) and their guidance compounds. When confidence is Low or Medium, widen the A/B/C spread on peak inlet to improve the chance that one of the three batches lands in a usable zone even if the anchor hypothesis is wrong.

### Precedence table — categorical decisions

| Triggering Condition | Categorical Decision | Why precedence (not additive) |
|---|---|---|
| **Process family — heavy ferment / anaerobic / XO present** | Anchor on the heavy-ferment family profile (Mandela XO #139), NOT the washed family profile. Density-driven anchor selection subordinates to fermentation cellulose's thermal behavior. | Anchor selection is a categorical pick from a discrete set; you don't sum two anchor choices. Shape per [CONTEXT-roasting.md § Signal precedence](CONTEXT-roasting.md). |
| **FC-audibility expectation — silent / subtle / ambiguous / did_not_fire predicted** | End condition is `bean_temp` at the expected drop target (~203-205°C for XO-type coffees, ~207°C for heavy-anaerobic Gesha), NOT `dev_time`. Dev-time end conditions assume an FC mark exists to count from; when FC is not audible (or never fires) the mark is unreliable and `dev_time` fires on whatever timer value was set, often past the drop ceiling (confirmed: Mandela XO V4 reached 51-58% Maillard when dev time fired at machine-estimated FC). All four non-audible enum values route to the same `bean_temp` regime; `did_not_fire` (5th value added Group 3 / Item 31 / migration 066 / 2026-05-24) covers the underdeveloped-low-energy-probe case where the bean tops out below the FC window (e.g. Bukure v2a, Mt Elgon batch 199). | End-condition mechanism is a categorical choice between two discrete regimes. Shape per [CONTEXT-roasting.md § Signal precedence](CONTEXT-roasting.md). |
| **Fruit-layer presence — visible mucilage / dried fruit layer on natural** | Energy-direction adjustment **inverts** vs density prediction — fruit-layer naturals need MORE energy than density predicts to get FC timing into range, not less. The fermentation cellulose / fruit layer provides thermal insulation that requires additional energy to drive FC. (Timing fix only; the flavor-quality hedge in the additive table runs the *opposite* direction once timing is in range.) | The sign of the energy adjustment categorically flips; you can't sum "more energy" and "less energy" and average them. Shape per [CONTEXT-roasting.md § Signal precedence](CONTEXT-roasting.md). |
| **Variety difficulty — documented atypical thermal/structural behavior (e.g. Daterra Laurina's pointy small beans)** | Anchor confidence drops independently of anchor-match quality — variety difficulty caps the confidence read even when process / cultivar / density all match. | Confidence grading is a categorical read; a documented-difficult variety vetoes the match-derived level rather than summing with it. |
| **Dev compression approaching the drop ceiling** | Hold the drop ceiling and accept compressed dev — do NOT chase FC timing through the ceiling (the FC-temp-architectural-constraint pattern; see [§ FC-Temp Architectural Constraint on Naturals](#fc-temp-architectural-constraint-on-naturals---working-hypothesis)). | Breaching the ceiling is irreversible cup damage; ceiling-vs-chase is a discrete either/or, not a summable hedge. |
| **High lot value + low anchor confidence** | Narrow the V1 spread (cost of a wide miss is high) and channel the confidence-driven widening pressure into pre-V1 risk reduction (density gating, paired roasted-reference cupping, producer outreach, deferral) instead of spread width. | A layering, not a head-on precedence: the two signals adjust DIFFERENT parameters. Per [CONTEXT-roasting.md § Pre-V1 risk reduction](CONTEXT-roasting.md). |
| **One-shot lot whose only available anchor is itself unresolved** | Defer the one-shot until the anchor's process family resolves — do not proceed against unresolved-anchor risk. | Proceed-vs-defer is categorical; an N=1 lot has no recovery batch to absorb an anchor miss. |

**How to use the precedence table:** When a triggering condition is present, the categorical decision wins outright — it does not stack with additive-table guidance on the same parameter. Walk the precedence table first; let it pick the anchor / end-condition / energy-direction; then walk the additive table for continuous adjustments inside that regime.

_Single-lot extension hypotheses (Low confidence - altitude proxy for one-shot calibrations, etc.) live in [§ Working Hypotheses (Single-Lot, Low Confidence)](#working-hypotheses-single-lot-low-confidence)._

**Low-density counterflow evidence lands (PAN-DEBORAH-GEISHA-NAT-2026, 741 g/L, closed 2026-09-07) - upgrades the additive table's 'Density <= 760' row from Low toward Medium confidence, with a mechanism refinement.** The first fully resolved low-density lot confirms the taper direction but relocates WHERE the adjustment matters: the -2C peak reduction was secondary; the dominant effect was that the low-density bean RACES the bean-temp probe - every batch dropped fast and 13-18 WB Agtron points LIGHTER than the model derived from denser family lots (786-809 g/L), so a given bean-temp trigger is a far lighter development point than the same number on a dense lot. The Agtron-per-degree-of-drop slope also steepened sharply above the FC zone (prediction error widening 3/6/7 points up a 210.5/212/213.5C ladder). Operational rule: on density <= ~760 g/L, do NOT transfer bean-temp trigger numbers or color models from denser lots even within the same process family - re-anchor both from the lot's own first bracket, and expect the playable zone at HIGHER trigger numbers but LIGHTER color than family precedent. Also a terroir caution: this lot was low-density at ~1,900 masl - do not infer density from elevation on engineered-process lots; measure it.

**Brew recipe gap:** There is consistently a significant delta between the Day 7 xbloom evaluation recipe and the optimized brew recipe. The evaluation recipe is calibrated for comparison consistency, not expression maximization. Always run an optimized brew session before declaring a lot resolved - the evaluation recipe will typically under-extract delicate washed coffees with unusual aromatic profiles. Lower temperature (91°C vs. 94°C) and higher concentration (1:14 vs. 1:17) were the primary levers on Sudan Rume Washed.

**Working hypothesis added (Low confidence, 1 lot - ECU-TD24-RANCHOTIO-TM-WASHED):** Underdeveloped roasts may have a warm-shifted peak evaluation window. Batch #179 (Maillard 49.4%, weight loss 11.89%, drop 203.3°C - structurally underdeveloped) peaked at 51-53°C on the Day 7 pourover under Clarity-First. As the cup cooled past peak it "almost became not as good" - degraded rather than continuing to integrate further (the opposite of the typical Clarity-First confirmation pattern, where cups peak 45-50°C and improve as they cool through that band). Hypothesis: underdeveloped roasts lack the late-Maillard caramelization compounds that carry the cup through deep cooling, so the peak window sits in the warm-mid band where the front-and-mid aromatics that DID develop are most expressive. Don't conclude cup is degrading at the standard cool-window if the roast came out underdeveloped. Treat as Low confidence pending 1-2 more underdeveloped-roast data points.

## xbloom Evaluation Gate Misranking on Engineered-Process Naturals

## FC-Temp Architectural Constraint on Naturals - Working Hypothesis

**Working hypothesis (Medium confidence, 2 lots - CGLE-SRUME-NATURAL-2026 + BRA-FAZENDAUM-WUSHWUSH-NAT-2026):** On natural-process coffees in counterflow, FC temperature appears anchored to 204-206°C across a wide range of peak inlet variation (242-251°C tested), regardless of cultivar, density, moisture, or terroir. Peak inlet variation pulls FC TIME earlier when raised but does NOT pull FC TEMP lower. The result: drop ceiling discipline forces dev floor failure (drop fires immediately after FC), or dev requirement forces drop ceiling breach.

Evidence:
- CGLE-SRUME-NATURAL-2026 V2 (242-248°C peak): FC temps 204-206°C across all three batches, dev compressed to 14-40s.
- BRA-FAZENDAUM-WUSHWUSH-NAT-2026 V1 (245-251°C peak): FC temps 204.7-205.6°C across all three batches, dev 23-42s. Replicates exact failure pattern on different cultivar (Wush Wush vs Sudan Rume), different terroir (Mantiqueira/Sul de Minas vs Western Andean Cordillera), different density (809 vs 791), different moisture (9.10% vs 10.30%), different drying (dark-room vs standard).

If this pattern confirms across a 3rd natural lot, the architectural fix is to abandon the peak-inlet-as-primary-V1-variable approach for naturals and design V1 around the FC-temp ceiling directly: either (a) push FC ARRIVAL to 200-202°C via early-ramp restructuring, or (b) switch end-condition to bean-temp like Mandela XO and accept FC at ~205°C as a measurement artifact, or (c) treat the SR Natural V3/V4 low-energy slow-bake as the architectural template for naturals generally, not as a SR-Natural-flavor-specific approach. Promote from hypothesis to confirmed pattern after a third natural lot exhibits the same FC-temp anchoring.

**BRA-FAZENDAUM-WUSHWUSH-NAT-2026 closed 2026-08-10 - constraint confirmed 3-for-3 across V-sets on this lot, now on the audible side.** V3 (batches 220/221/222) all crossed FC audibly at 205.5-205.8°C on v2c's parent curve, after V1 found FC at 204.7-205.6°C across a 245-251°C peak sweep and V2's three 242°C-peak batches failed to reach FC at all at 200/202/204°C drops. Net: on this lot FC TEMP was fixed at ~205°C across every profile shape tested (peak inlet moved FC TIME only; gentler early ramp moved nothing). The lot resolved by treating the fixed FC as an anchor: bean-temp auto-drop at 206°C (1°C above FC) produced the FC-onset reference roast (Batch 220). Design implication when the constraint holds: the drop ceiling is the entire development lever, and it acts nonlinearly through the slow post-FC climb (~1°C/30s on this curve; +2°C of ceiling bought +57s of dev) - bracket ceilings in 1°C steps, not 2°C.

### Altitude as weak proxy for density on one-shot calibrations (ECU-TD24-RANCHOTIO-TM-WASHED, observed 2026-05)

When density is unmeasured, altitude is a WEAK proxy for the energy-adjustment direction: a -2°C peak-inlet hedge for a 1,300 m lot produced underdevelopment (Batch 179 wanted MORE energy). Default for one-shots with unmeasured density: full anchor energy + 125°C hopper pre-load, no altitude-based downward hedge. Firms with 2+ more one-shot data points. ### v1c silent-FC profile may not be cleanly reproducible at identical inlet curve (COS-HIG-BOR-2026, observed 2026-05)

Confirmed twice within the lot (v1c→v2a and v2b→v3a verbatim-profile replications both diverged - FC audibility flipped in both directions, WB-to-ground delta swung, cup expression changed). ### xbloom dark-tea ceiling on dark-tea-prone naturals - descriptor-family axis (COS-HIG-BOR-2026 V1 + RWA-NOVA-NAT21-RB-2026 V2, observed 2026-05/06)

### WB→Ground Agtron Delta Polarity as a Cup-Quality Diagnostic (Single-lot working hypothesis, REDPLUM-CAS-2026 V1+V2+SPG)

On REDPLUM-CAS-2026 (washed + pulp ferment, 14 cuppings across four V-sets) the WB→Ground delta POLARITY predicted cup quality more reliably than any single roast-side metric: **tight delta (≤1)** → integrated cup; **large positive delta (+3 to +5)** → interior ahead of surface, reads as stall-hollow when the dev path stalled OR as built body when it was extended-gentle (V4's 211/212, the reference landed at +3.3) - so positive magnitude needs dev-path context; **negative delta (ground LIGHTER than WB)** → surface-blast, a disintegrated cup brewing does not rescue (198, 205). The SIGN is the robust signal. Mechanism (provisional): surface heated faster than the interior can follow - on the L200 at 100 g most reliably from high peak inlet + fast bean-temp climb into a low BEAN_TEMP end condition. It is a diagnostic, not a design lever.

**Second-lot data point (BRA-FAZENDAUM-WUSHWUSH-NAT-2026, closed 2026-08-10) - PARTIAL support with a scope nuance.** Sign inversion recurred 4x but always on the lightest batch(es) of each set, and the 4th sat on Batch 220, which became the reference roast and delivered the full producer-notes cup. On that lot the inversion reads as a light-end FC-onset quirk, not a kill signal. Refinement candidate: negative delta on a DEVELOPED batch (RedPlum) vs at the FC-onset light edge (Wush Wush) may be different phenomena. **Promotion criterion:** record WB + ground Agtron at Day-7 cupping routinely; a third lot showing either pattern cleanly arbitrates. Detail: [learnings/redplum-cas-2026.md](docs/skills/roasting-historian/cluster/learnings/redplum-cas-2026.md) + [learnings/bra-fazendaum-wushwush-nat-2026.md](docs/skills/roasting-historian/cluster/learnings/bra-fazendaum-wushwush-nat-2026.md).

### Late-phase inlet slope as the body-balance lever for hollow-middle washed lots (REDPLUM-CAS-2026, resolved 2026-06-22)

Four V-sets of bright-attack / hollow-middle on a washed pulp-ferment Castillo; peak inlet, front-half shape and drop ceiling all failed, and **flattening the post-FC inlet slope** (lower the 04:00 point, raise the 06:00 floor, hold peak + BEAN_TEMP 207°C) built the missing body without darkening. Two cautions: the lever is entangled with a later FC (lowering 04:00 pulls pre-FC energy - move a LATER point to isolate it), and it is robust across dev path (64s and 35s dev hit the same cup). **Confidence: single-lot, Low.** Promotion criterion: design the flatter post-FC slope from the outset on the next hollow-middle-prone washed lot and confirm. Full arc + the session-position control note: [learnings/redplum-cas-2026.md § Carry-forward](docs/skills/roasting-historian/cluster/learnings/redplum-cas-2026.md).
